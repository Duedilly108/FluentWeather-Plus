FluentWeather-Plus is FluentWeather 2.1.0 by Gabboxl (github.com/Gabboxl/FluentWeather) with the additions in this file. It keeps all features of the original app. Part 1 of this file lists everything FluentWeather-Plus adds since FluentWeather 2.1.0. Part 2 lists the features of the original FluentWeather 2.1.0. Its version is the last part of the build number, so build 2.1.0.49 is v49. Each build on a PC adds one. Each PC builds and signs its own copy with the files in the LocalBuild folder. LocalBuild/README.txt has the setup steps.


Part 1. Features that FluentWeather-Plus adds
=============================================

The app window has these features.

A star button next to the location name saves the location that is shown.
Clicking the star again removes the location.
Up to 25 locations can be saved.
Saved locations appear as buttons below the location name.
Clicking a location button switches to that location.
Right-clicking a location button opens a menu to move it left, move it right or remove it.
Ctrl+1 to Ctrl+9 switch to saved locations 1 to 9.
Ctrl+Tab and Ctrl+Shift+Tab switch to the next or previous saved location.
Two saved locations with the same short name show their full names.
Weather data for each location stays in memory for 10 minutes. Switching back to a recent location shows it at once without a network request.
A slow response for an earlier location never replaces the location picked last.
After the app wakes up, its first request can go over a connection that the network already closed. A request that fails before the server answers is sent once more on a new connection. Idle connections close after 15 seconds.
A tip below the location buttons explains the star button. With no saved locations the tip is always shown. With saved locations a close button hides it for good.
The setting "Text and picture size" under the gear button sets the size of the weather area from 50 to 100 percent in 5 percent steps, with a minus button, a plus button and a 100 percent button. The default is 100 percent.
Ctrl+Minus makes the weather area 5 percent smaller. Ctrl+Plus makes it 5 percent larger. Ctrl+0 returns it to 100 percent.
The setting "Background contrast" makes the text easier to read on the weather background photo. Original shows the photo as it is. Dark and Darker shade the photo and keep light text. Light and Lighter brighten the photo and switch the page to dark text. Dark is the default.

The setting "Card tint" tints the cards over the photo, so their text stays readable on bright or busy photos. A slider sets it from 0 to 100 percent in 5 percent steps. 0 percent keeps the original look. 40 percent is the default. The tint is dark with light text and white with the light background contrasts. The selected day keeps its dark fill.

The "Radar" section at the bottom of the page shows a radar map of the area around the location. It appears only for US locations, including Alaska, Hawaii, Puerto Rico and Guam. The radar and the state and county borders come from NOAA and the National Weather Service. The terrain comes from the USGS National Map. All three are free public US government services and need no key. The + and - buttons zoom from level 4 to level 10, and the app keeps the zoom. The map refreshes with the weather data. It is dark, and light with the light background contrasts. A dot marks the location. The line below the map shows the time of the last update. It also says when there is no rain or snow in the area, and when the radar could not be loaded.
With Light or Lighter the cards, the titles and the window buttons also turn dark.
The setting "Hourly forecast" shows the hours of the selected day, or 24, 36 or 48 hours. Day is the default.
For today the 24, 36 and 48 hour lists start at the current hour. For another day they start at midnight of that day.
In those lists the first hour and each midnight show the weekday.
The setting "Language" sets the language of the app text and of the weather descriptions. "Windows default" uses the Windows display language. The Language button opens a window with a scrollable list of every language that comes with the app.
After a language change the app offers to restart.
The search box reads "Location search" in a slightly smaller font, so it fits the narrowest window.
The top bar fits narrow windows. The refresh button, the search box and the settings button never cover the app name or the window buttons. In a narrow window the app name is hidden.
The saved locations tip wraps to the width of the window.
The settings panel under the gear button is opaque, with a gradient from dark grey at the top to blue grey at the bottom. Its text is always light. It fills the window height down to a small gap at the bottom. The sections sit in three columns of 440, 400 and 380 pixels. The credits sit in a card below the last section of the left column. The middle column ends with "Nearby water". The right column holds "More data options", which lists the five Open-Meteo feeds and the 13 "More data" items under one heading, with one hint at the end. On Windows 10 1903 and later the panel may extend past the app window, so the number of columns follows the screen width. With less than 1360 pixels it uses two columns, with the right column below the middle one. With less than 940 pixels it uses one column. Units and time format share one row, and so do acrylic effects and background image. In a short window the panel scrolls.
The credits in the settings panel list "FluentWeather-Plus v49 updated by duedilly108" first. The second line reads "Based on FluentWeather v2.1.0 developed by Gabboxl". The version link, for example v49, opens the releases page, where each version can be downloaded. The "GitHub project" link opens the FluentWeather-Plus repository with its source code.
The app icon sits in the top left corner of the window, with a small blue "P" badge. Hovering over the icon shows the app name, the version and the build number, for example "FluentWeather-Plus v49" and "Build 2.1.0.49". Hovering over the refresh button shows "Refresh", and hovering over the gear button shows "Settings".
Tooltips use the same grey to blue grey gradient as the settings panel.
The settings panel has a tray icons button next to the language button. It reads "Hide tray icons" while the tray icons run and "Show tray icons" when they do not. "Show tray icons" starts them, also after "Exit tray icons" in the tray menu. "Hide tray icons" makes them exit. Setup registers the link type fluentweather-tray: for the tray program, and the button opens fluentweather-tray:start or fluentweather-tray:stop. Windows may ask once whether to switch apps. While the tray icons run, they write the time to tray-status.txt in the app data folder every 30 seconds and delete the file when they exit. The app reads that file to label the button. If the tray icons are not installed, the button shows "Not installed" for a few seconds.
The "Auto refresh" setting uses option buttons for Off, 15 minutes, 30 minutes and 1 hour. A saved setting of 5 or 10 minutes from an earlier version becomes 15 minutes.
The list of locations below the search box stays at least 340 pixels wide in a narrow window. Long names wrap onto a second line.
The "Updated on" line shows the date in the Windows date format and the time in the 12 or 24 hour setting of the app.
The app writes errors it catches to crash.log in its data folder. The file stays under 256 KB and is never sent anywhere. "FluentWeather crash report" in the Start menu writes FluentWeather-crash-report.txt on the Desktop with that file, the FluentWeather entries of the Windows event logs and the Windows crash reports of the app from the last 3 days, and opens it in Notepad. It reads local files only.
The setting "Weather data source" chooses where the weather comes from. Automatic is the default. It uses weather.com, and when weather.com refuses the app's key twice in a row it switches to Open-Meteo. A lost internet connection never causes the switch. On Open-Meteo it tries weather.com again once an hour and switches back when weather.com answers. "weather.com" never switches. "Open-Meteo" always uses Open-Meteo. While Open-Meteo is in use, the line "Data: Open-Meteo" shows under the update time. Open-Meteo gives the current conditions and the hourly and daily forecasts. The app calculates the 24-hour totals and extremes, the pressure trend, heat index, wind chill, the moon phase and the moonrise and moonset times, the day and night parts with their text, and the running, driving, dry skin and watering insights. The app also reads the nearest airport weather report (METAR) from the Aviation Weather Center of the US National Weather Service at aviationweather.gov. It is free, needs no key and covers airports worldwide. The app uses the nearest station within 50 km that has a report from the last 2 hours, and asks once per location every 30 minutes. The report gives the cloud ceiling, the visibility, the pressure and the station for the tray line "Nearest station". A station within 25 km also gives the measured temperature, dew point, humidity, wind and gusts. The line under the update time then reads "Data: Open-Meteo and station" with the station code, for example KFFL. Without a station in range, the ceiling shows "not available". The US pollen stays empty, and the forecast text is in English. Location search uses the Open-Meteo place search. The app remembers the name and coordinates of each place whenever weather.com answers, so saved locations keep their names on Open-Meteo. The tray refresh does this for every saved location within about 15 minutes.
After an update the app shows a "What's new in FluentWeather-Plus" window with a FluentWeather-Plus banner, the version number of the build, a credit to FluentWeather 2.1.0 by Gabboxl, and the list of features that FluentWeather-Plus adds. Its link opens the releases page of FluentWeather-Plus on GitHub.
The window opens at 820 by 600 pixels the first time. After that Windows remembers the size and position the user sets.
The app accepts the link fluentweather-local:switch?placeId=<id> and opens at that location. It accepts saved locations only. With "&alerts=open" added, the app also opens the details of the first alert. The tray alert bubble uses this link.

"Past days" shows the last two days left of today in the daily forecast, titled "Yesterday" and with the weekday and date of the day before. The app records each saved location twice a day: about an hour after the low and about an hour after the high that the hourly forecast predicts. Each capture is one small request. The current conditions hold the measured lowest temperature of the last 24 hours and the measured highest temperature since 7 AM, so a late capture still records the right values. A background task checks every 15 minutes which captures are due, so the window does not need to be open. Each PC adds a random delay of 0 to 20 minutes, and a failed capture waits an hour before the next try. The record keeps 366 days, for the history window. A year of one location takes about 60 KB. Recording starts when the app is installed, so "Yesterday" appears the next day. A past day card always shows the day icon. A day that was first recorded in the evening only has the night forecast, so the app shows the day version of its icon. Scroll left from today to see the past days. The day row has a scroll bar for free scrolling, and it settles on the nearest whole card when the scrolling stops. Each mouse wheel notch over the row moves it one card, and so do the left and right arrow keys. Home and End go to the ends.

The detail cards start in this order: Wind, Humidity, UV index, Air quality, Nearby water and the extra Open-Meteo cards (when turned on), Visibility, Pressure and Dew point. Hovering over a card for 1.2 seconds shows more detail in a panel with the same look as the app tooltips. Wind shows the speed, the direction in degrees, the gusts and the wind chill. When weather.com reports a gust, the Wind card itself also shows it on a second line, for example "gusts to 18". Humidity shows a comfort word, the heat index and the dew point. UV index shows advice for its level. Visibility shows a word for it and the cloud ceiling. weather.com sends the ceiling in feet for every unit setting, so the app converts it to meters for metric units. Pressure shows the trend, the change in 3 hours and what a rising or falling pressure often means. With US units the Pressure card shows inHg, as US forecasts do. It showed the mb value with an "hg" label before. Dew point shows a comfort word from dry to oppressive and the gap to the temperature. A card can be dragged to a new place in the row. Right-clicking a card opens a menu with Move left, Move right, Move to start, Move to end, Hide this card, Add card and Reset cards. "Add card" lists every card that is not in the row, each with a few words about it in dim text, and the added card goes right after the card that was clicked. Nearby water and the extra Open-Meteo cards also have "Card settings", which opens the settings panel. Hiding or adding Nearby water or an extra Open-Meteo card also changes its switch or "Main" box in the settings panel. "Reset cards" puts the standard cards back in their first order. One layout serves every location and is kept between runs. A card that hides because a location has no data keeps its place. In a narrow window they scroll sideways, with a scroll bar below them. The "Air quality" card follows the UV index card. It shows the index and its category, for example "22 Good". US locations use the US EPA scale, and other locations use the European scale. The tooltip shows the PM2.5, PM10, ozone and nitrogen dioxide readings. The data comes from the free Open-Meteo air quality API, which needs no key and uses no weather.com request. Its source is the Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service, licensed CC BY 4.0, and the tooltip names it. Each location is downloaded at most once an hour, because the values change hourly. The card stays hidden when no data is available.

The first five rows of "More data options" in the settings panel list five Open-Meteo feeds, each with a "Main" box and a "Tray" box. All are off by default. "Main" adds a card after the Air quality card. "Tray" writes a line for the tray hover panel, which is then ticked under Hover details in the tray menu. "Waves and sea temperature" shows the wave height and period, the swell and the sea surface temperature from the Open-Meteo marine model, for points on or near the ocean. "Wind forecast (gusts, 80 m)" shows the highest gust of the next 6 hours, for example "22 mph" with "top gust, next 6 hours" below it. Its tooltip adds the wind at 80 m. It does not repeat the current wind and gusts, which the Wind card shows from weather.com. The Nearby water card shows a third wind, the one measured at the water station, when the station has a wind sensor. "Other forecast models" shows today's high, low and precipitation from the ECMWF, GFS and ICON models. A wide spread means the forecast is uncertain. "River flow" shows the modeled discharge of the nearest river today and what is usual for the day (GloFAS), in cubic feet or cubic meters per second. "Pollen" shows the weather.com tree, grass and ragweed levels from none to very high where weather.com has them, which includes the US, with the levels for today, tonight and tomorrow in its tooltip. The weather requests of the app already carry these levels, so they add no request. Elsewhere the card shows six pollen types from Open-Meteo, in Europe only. Each card hides when the location has no data. The data is free from open-meteo.com under CC BY 4.0, except the US pollen, which comes with the weather.com data. Each tooltip names its source. Each location and feed is requested at most once an hour.

Severe weather alerts show in a colored band under the location name, one band per alert, with the name of the alert and the time it ends, for example "Winter Storm Warning until Fri 6:00 PM". Warnings are red, watches orange, advisories dark yellow and statements blue. A click on a band opens the full text of the alert below it, in a panel with the gradient of the settings panel. The panel shows the issuing office, the time of issue, the end time, the area and the text. The text comes as fixed-width lines, so the app joins the lines of each paragraph and turns "* WHAT...Heavy snow" into "WHAT: Heavy snow". A second click closes the panel. Up to 4 bands show. More alerts show as "and 2 more". The alerts come from weather.com, which passes on the alerts of the national weather services, such as the US National Weather Service and Environment Canada. When the app uses the Open-Meteo backup, or weather.com does not answer, US locations get their alerts directly from the National Weather Service at api.weather.gov. Each location is asked at most every 5 minutes. The setting "Alerts in the main window" shows all alerts, warnings and watches, or warnings only. All alerts is the default.

The next 13 rows of "More data options" list the "More data" items, each with a "Main" box and a "Tray" box. "Severe weather alerts" is on by default. The other items are off by default. "Main" shows the item in the main window. "Tray" writes a line for the tray hover panel, which is then ticked under Hover details in the tray menu. Hovering over the name of an item shows its source. "Day details" and "Forecaster's discussion" have no tray line, because they are too long for the hover panel.
"Weather now (airport)" adds a line to the Visibility card with the weather and the flight category of the nearest airport report within 50 km, for example "light snow, mist, MVFR (KFFL)". The weather codes of the report are written out in words. The hover text adds the cloud ceiling at the airport, the airport forecast (TAF) in short lines such as "Fri 9:00 AM: 3 mi, light snow, ceiling 2,400 ft", and both reports as sent. Small airports have no forecast, so the forecast comes from the nearest airport that has one. The data comes from the Aviation Weather Center at aviationweather.gov.
"Snow, last 1, 6 and 24 h" adds a card with the snow of the last 6 hours, and the last hour and 24 hours below it, from weather.com.
"Heat stress (WBGT)" adds a card with the wet bulb globe temperature from weather.com and a level from low to extreme. Work and sports guidelines use this value in hot weather.
"Storm energy and thunder risk" adds a card with the storm energy (CAPE) now in J/kg and a level from very low to extreme, and the weather.com thunder risk of today from 0 to 5. The hover text adds the highest storm energy of today. The storm energy comes from Open-Meteo.
"Soil and snow depth" adds a card with the soil temperature at 6 cm, the soil moisture at 3 to 9 cm and the snow depth, from the Open-Meteo model.
"Freezing level" adds a card with the height of the 0 degree level above sea level from Open-Meteo. When the air is below freezing from the ground up, the card shows "At ground".
"Sunshine and evaporation" adds a card with the hours of sunshine today and the hours of daylight. The hover text adds the solar radiation now and the evaporation of today (FAO-56 reference evapotranspiration), from Open-Meteo.
"Buoy: waves, air and tide" adds a card for the nearest NOAA buoy or tide station within 50 km with a report from the last 3 hours. It shows the wave height, the wave period and the direction the waves come from. A tide station without waves shows the tide. The hover text adds the air and water temperature, the pressure with its 3 hour change, and the tide.
"River gauge (US)" adds a card for the nearest USGS river gauge within 40 km. It shows the height and the flow, whether the river is rising, falling or steady in the last 3 hours, and the mean height of each of the last 7 days as small bars. A gauge that also measures the flow comes before a lake or reservoir level. The hover text adds the name of the gauge, the change of the last 3 hours and the lowest and highest daily mean of the week.
"Day details: thunder, snow, wind" adds a mark to the day buttons when weather.com expects snow, for example "1-3 in", or a thunder risk of 1 or more, for example "3/5". The day details below the hourly forecast get the wind text of the day and night, the forecast notes, the amounts of rain, snow and ice, and the thunder risk. A snow range from weather.com shows in place of the snow amount.
"Hourly chart, next 12 h in tray" adds a chart under the hourly forecast for the same hours as the list. It shows stacked bars of rain, snow and ice per hour, the chance of precipitation as a dashed line, the sunshine of each hour as a yellow bar along the top, and a red mark on hours that weather.com rates as severe. Snow is drawn as water, 10 to 1, so it does not hide the rain. The hover text gives the total of the hours shown. The tray line gives the rain, snow and ice of the next 12 hours and the highest chance, for example "Dry, chance 7%".
"Forecaster's discussion (US)" adds a section below the radar map with the latest Area Forecast Discussion of the local National Weather Service office. It shows the first 8 lines, and "Show all" shows the rest. The app removes the message header and joins the fixed-width lines into paragraphs.
The cards of "More data" go after the extra Open-Meteo cards and work like them. They can be dragged, hidden and added with the card menu, and the card menu changes their "Main" box. Each card hides when the location has no data. Each source is asked at most every 30 minutes per location.

In the evening weather.com no longer sends the daytime high of today. The day card then shows the high of the whole calendar day, where it showed "--" before.

A "Full History" button with an arrow sits at the right end of the "Daily forecast" title. It shows the history of one saved location over the main window, below the title bar. The page title is centered. The "Back" button at the left and the Esc key close it. The page has a location list, a year list, a month list and a Month or Year choice. The chart covers the chosen year. Month fits it to the chosen month and Year to the whole year. The mouse wheel zooms in and out around the pointer. Shift with the wheel, a sideways swipe on a touchpad, dragging the chart, or the scroll bar below the chart move the view through the year. The minus and plus buttons zoom without a wheel, and Fit shows the whole year. A view of 26 days or more shows one point per day. A shorter view shows every hour, down to a single day. A row of chart buttons chooses the chart: Temperature, Wind, Rain and snow, Air quality, Humidity, Pressure, and Clouds and sun. The day views show one point per day. Temperature shows the daily high in orange and the low in blue with a shaded band between them. Wind shows the highest gust and the highest wind of each day, with a wind barb for the main direction below the days, spaced so they do not overlap. Rain and snow shows bars. Air quality shows the highest US AQI of the day for US locations and the highest European AQI elsewhere, from August 2022. Humidity shows the mean humidity and the mean dew point. Pressure shows the mean sea level pressure. Clouds and sun shows the mean cloud cover and bars for the hours of sunshine. Lines have square markers and value labels when the points are far enough apart. A run of days without data is bridged with a dashed grey line. The hourly view has the same chart buttons, with night hours shaded and wind barbs spaced so they do not overlap. Clicking a day zooms to that day. Esc first goes back to the view before the click, then back to the month or year, then closes the page. A dotted line under the pointer marks the day or hour, and a panel beside it shows its values. The table below the chart lists every field of the part in view, newest day first, or hour by hour. Its rows are built only when they scroll into view. In a narrow window the pickers and the table scroll sideways. The days the app recorded are marked "recorded". The other days come from the Open-Meteo historical weather archive and are marked "archive". The archive is a weather model (ERA5 from the Copernicus Climate Change Service), free for non-commercial use under CC BY 4.0, and its values can differ a little from the recorded values. It publishes a day about five days after it ends, and the hourly view uses the Open-Meteo forecast data for the last week. A recorded value always wins over the archive. The app downloads one month of one location per request and keeps it in its data folder. The days of the whole year load when the page opens, the shown month first. The hours load one month at a time, only for the months in a short view. One month of hours is about 50 KB to download. A complete month is never downloaded again. One with missing days is downloaded again when its copy is more than a day old. Only the part in view is drawn, so a year of hours stays light on a small laptop. The archive goes back to 1940. The page needs the coordinates of the location, which the app stores the first time it shows a saved location. All values follow the units setting of the app. The credit line at the bottom of the page names the sources.

The "Nearby water" section of the settings panel has two switches, both off by default. "Card in the main window" adds a card two chips wide after the Air quality card. "Line in the tray hover panel" adds two lines to the tray data. The card shows the water temperature at the nearest water station, the wind, and a line with the station name, its distance and the age of the reading. The wind is the station's own wind when the station has a wind sensor, otherwise the Open-Meteo wind of the location, and the tooltip says which. The tooltip also lists the Open-Meteo gusts now, the highest gust of the next 6 hours, the wind at 80 m, and on the ocean the waves, the swell and the modeled sea surface temperature. The tooltip also names the source, the station id, the reading time, the air temperature and the wave height at the station when it has them, and the credit. The stations come from two free public US government services that need no key. The NOAA National Data Buoy Center (NDBC) covers buoys and shore stations on the coasts and the Great Lakes, including the NOAA tide stations, and often has wind, air temperature and wave height at the same station. One NDBC file of about 100 KB holds the latest reading of every station, and the app downloads it at most every 30 minutes. Great Lakes buoys are taken out of the water in winter. USGS Water Data covers rivers and some lakes and reservoirs with water temperature only. The app uses the closest station within 50 km (31 mi) with a reading under 6 hours old. NDBC stations are tried first, then USGS. On the ocean with no station in range, the card shows the sea surface temperature of the Open-Meteo marine model at the location and says so. The card hides when there is none. Each location is looked up at most once every 30 minutes. The NDBC station names are downloaded once a month, and USGS station names are looked up once each and kept. Small inland lakes often have no station, so the card may show a river gauge nearby.


The app writes weather data for the tray icons.

The app writes the current conditions of the location it shows to a small file in its own data folder.
It writes the current conditions of every saved location to a second file.
The app background task refreshes both files about every 15 minutes, also with the app window closed.
Saved locations refresh at most every 10 minutes, with up to 4 requests at the same time.
Each saved location uses a small request of about 6 KB. It includes the 3-day forecast for the moonrise and moonset times. If that request fails, the app sends it again without the forecast.
A failed request keeps the last data for that location.
Each file write replaces the old file in one step, so a reader never sees half a file.
For each location the file also holds the place id and, when "Severe weather alerts" has its Tray box ticked, one line per active alert with its key, type, end time, name, area, source and summary. The key stays the same when the weather service updates an alert, so an update does not count as a new alert.
The "More data" items with a ticked Tray box add their lines too, for example "d.MoreGauge=4.14 ft, 165 ft3/s, steady".


The tray icons have these features.

Two icons appear side by side in the notification area. The left icon shows the temperature as a number. The right icon shows the weather picture for the current conditions.
The weather picture is one of 15. Sunny shows a full sun. Clear night shows the moon. Partly cloudy shows a sun partly behind a cloud. Partly cloudy night shows the moon partly behind a cloud. Cloudy shows a cloud. Cloudy night shows the lit edge of the moon behind a cloud. Fog shows horizontal bands. Light rain shows a cloud with 2 rain drops. Rain shows a cloud with 4 rain drops. Heavy rain shows a dark cloud with 7 rain drops. Ice or mixed shows a cloud with rain drops and snowflakes. Thunderstorm shows a lightning bolt with 7 rain drops. Severe storm shows a lightning bolt with a red warning triangle. Light snow shows 3 snowflakes. Heavy snow shows 7 snowflakes.
The moon shows its phase for today. North of the equator a waxing moon is lit on the right and a waning moon on the left. South of the equator the sides are swapped.
The moon is drawn in a light brown.
weather.com sends the same cloudy code by day and by night. The app writes whether it is day or night, so the cloudy picture shows the moon at night.
In the cloudy night picture the moon shows on its lit side. At 16 to 32 pixels more of the moon shows, so it stays visible.
The tray program draws the pictures itself, so they stay sharp at every display scale.
In the rain pictures the cloud fills the top 40 percent, so the drops are easy to count at 16 pixels.
The picture follows the weather code and the condition text. When the text says light, heavy or thunder, the picture shows that strength. When the code shows only a cloud, sun or moon and the text names rain, snow, ice or thunder, the text decides. "Showers in the Vicinity" with a cloud code shows light rain. This works for English condition text. In other languages the weather code alone decides.
The number can show the temperature or the feels-like temperature.
The icons follow the light or dark taskbar theme.
The number shows "--" when there is no data or the data is older than 3 hours.
The icons update within a second of a change in the data files.
Left-clicking an icon opens the app at the location the tray shows.
Left-clicking an icon again while the app window is the active window closes the window, so only the tray icons stay. A window behind other windows comes to the front instead.
The right-click menu picks the location the tray shows. It lists each saved location with its temperature, plus "Same city as the app". The app window stays closed.
Hovering over an icon shows a panel with the location name and the chosen details.
The panel opens only when the pointer stays on the icons for the whole 1.2 seconds. If the pointer leaves sooner, nothing opens. Each new visit starts a new 1.2 seconds.
The panel shows a detailed version of the weather picture with a soft glow behind it.
The panel background runs from very dark blue at the top to dark blue at the bottom. It stretches with the number of details shown. The text is white with pale blue labels.
The panel has rounded corners and a soft shadow. It has one fixed width of 340 pixels at 100 percent display scale. A long location name, a long detail and the bottom line wrap onto more lines and are never cut off. The lines below the weather picture use the full width. The height follows the number of details.
The hover panel can be dragged with the left mouse button. It then opens at that place. A double-click on the panel puts it back next to the icons.
After the pointer leaves the icons and the panel, the panel stays 0.9 seconds more. This gives the pointer time to reach a panel at its saved place.
A click on the panel without a move does not change its place. A saved place that is off the screen, for example after a monitor was removed, moves back onto the screen.
The details to show are chosen in the "Hover details" submenu of the right-click menu. A large blue arrow marks that submenu. The choices are conditions, temperature, feels like, 24 hour high and low, 24 hour change, dew point, heat index, wind chill, humidity, wind and gusts, UV index, pressure and trend, visibility, cloud ceiling, rain for 1, 6 and 24 hours, snow for 24 hours, sunrise, sunset, moon phase, moonrise, moonset, observation time, nearest station, water temperature and wind at water station. "Show all" and "Reset to defaults" are in the same menu.
The hover detail "Moon phase" shows the phase name and the lit percentage, by day and by night. It is on by default.
The hover details "Moonrise" and "Moonset" show the next moonrise and the next moonset. A time on a later day starts with the weekday, for example "Thu 2:58 AM". They are off by default.
The hover detail "Nearest station" shows the closest official weather station with its code and distance, for example "Fairfield (KFFL), 3.1 mi". It is off by default. The app looks it up once per location and keeps it for 30 days. weather.com blends many sources for the current conditions of the exact location, so the station is shown for reference.
The hover details "Water temperature" and "Wind at water station" show the nearest water station, for example "56F, Des Moines River at Keosauqua, IA, 19 mi" and "12 mph N". They are off by default. The app writes these lines only when "Line in the tray hover panel" is on under "Nearby water" in the app settings.
The right-click menu also has "Restart FluentWeather (app and tray icons)", "Close FluentWeather window", "Exit tray icons" and "Exit tray icons and close FluentWeather".
"Close FluentWeather window" closes the window like its X button, so Windows remembers the window size and position. If the app still runs after 4 seconds, the tray program ends it.
The tray icons start at sign-in. The app window does not open at sign-in unless Setup runs with -StartApp.
The Start menu has "FluentWeather tray icons" and "Restart FluentWeather".
A new severe weather alert opens a bubble above the notification area. The bubble has the gradient, border, rounded corners and fonts of the hover panel, and a bar in the alert color on its left side. It shows the name of the alert, the location, the end time, the summary, the issuing office and "Show details". "Show details" opens the app at the location of the alert with the alert details open. The bubble never takes the focus from the window you work in.
The bubble closes after the time chosen under "Close the bubble after": never, 30 seconds, 1 minute, 5 minutes or 15 minutes. 5 minutes is the default. The bubble shows the time left, and the time stops while the pointer is on the bubble. The X button closes it at once.
"Honor Do Not Disturb (use Windows notifications)" shows a Windows notification in place of the bubble. Windows holds notifications back while Do Not Disturb is on and shows them in the notification center. A click on the notification opens the alert details. It is off by default.
"Show alerts" chooses Off, Warnings, Warnings and watches, or All alerts. Warnings and watches is the default. "Locations" chooses the city the tray shows or all saved locations. The city the tray shows is the default.
Each alert shows once. The tray program keeps the keys of shown alerts in tray-alerts-seen.txt in its own folder and drops each key a day after the alert ends. When several new alerts arrive at once, the bubble shows the most important one and "And 2 more alerts".
"Show a test alert" in the "Severe weather alerts" submenu shows a sample bubble with the current choices.
While the city the tray shows has an active alert that passes the "Show alerts" choice, the weather picture has a small warning triangle in the alert color in its lower right corner.
The Hover details submenu has new lines: Weather alert, Weather now (airport), Snow last 1 h and 6 h, Thunder risk today, Heat stress (WBGT), Freezing level, Soil temperature and moisture, Sunshine today, Buoy waves and tide, River height and flow, and Next 12 h rain, snow and ice. "Weather alert" is on by default, also for saved choices of an earlier version. The others show when their Tray box is ticked in the app.
The tray program never connects to the internet.
Each PC compiles the tray program from FluentWeatherTray.cs with the C# compiler that ships with Windows.


Setup has these features.

Setup needs no Microsoft account, no Microsoft Store and no winget.
Setup creates a code signing certificate for the PC. The private key stays in the user certificate store and cannot be exported. The certificate is valid for 5 years.
Windows trusts packages signed with that certificate on that PC only.
Setup installs Git for Windows and Visual Studio 2026 Build Tools if they are missing. It checks the digital signature of each installer before it runs it.
Setup uses installers that the user downloaded with a web browser into the Downloads folder. This works when a firewall blocks PowerShell.
Setup copies its files to C:\Program Files\FluentWeatherLocal, where only administrators can change them.
Setup adds "FluentWeather-Plus build tools" to Settings > Apps. Uninstalling it there removes the app, the tray icons, the certificate and all files. Setup.cmd -Uninstall does the same.
Setup can remove a Microsoft Store copy of FluentWeather with -RemoveStoreVersion.


Builds have these features.

The build always uses release 2.1.0 of FluentWeather. It does not check GitHub for newer releases, because a newer release can break the FluentWeather-Plus changes.
Setup removes the logon task of earlier versions, which checked for new releases every 30 days.
A new version of FluentWeather-Plus comes as a new package of the LocalBuild folder. Setup.cmd copies the new files and builds the new version.
"Update FluentWeather.cmd" -Force rebuilds the installed version.
A build downloads the source of release 2.1.0, applies the FluentWeather-Plus patch, builds, checks the signature and installs.
Saved locations and settings stay after a new build.
Each build gets its own version number, so Windows always accepts the new package.
A notification reports the result of each build.
The logs folder keeps the last 20 log files.


The network relay has these features.

During a build, git and MSBuild connect only to FluentWeatherNet.exe on 127.0.0.1.
The relay connects only to the host names in allowed-hosts.txt, and only on port 443.
The allowed hosts are github.com, api.github.com, api.nuget.org and globalcdn.nuget.org.
The relay logs each allowed and each blocked connection.
The relay runs only while a build runs and exits when the build ends.
A firewall needs exceptions for two programs only, FluentWeatherNet.exe and the FluentWeather-Plus app. Git, MSBuild and PowerShell need no exception.
Each PC compiles the relay from FluentWeatherNet.cs with the C# compiler that ships with Windows.


The single-file installer has these features.

"Make FluentWeather installer" in the Start menu builds FluentWeatherLocal-Installer.exe on the Desktop.
Each PC compiles the installer from FluentWeatherInstaller.cs with the C# compiler that ships with Windows. The build makes no internet connection.
The installer holds the setup files only. It holds no certificate, no settings, no saved locations and no logs.
When it runs, it asks to install now, to extract the files to a Desktop folder first, or to cancel.
An install extracts the files to a temporary folder, runs Setup, waits for it to finish and deletes the temporary folder.
Each PC that runs the installer creates its own signing certificate.


The direct installer has these features.

"Make FluentWeather direct installer" in the Start menu makes FluentWeather-Plus-Setup-v49.exe, FluentWeather-Plus-Uninstall-v49.exe and README.txt in a folder FluentWeather-Plus on the Desktop, plus a zip of the three files named FluentWeather-Plus-v49.zip, for build 2.1.0.49. The version is also in README.txt, in the title of the setup window and in Properties > Details of both programs.
It also writes FluentWeather-Plus-v49-release-notes.txt on the Desktop, outside the zip. That file holds the text for the GitHub release page from Release-notes.txt, with the VirusTotal links of the two new files filled in.
README.txt in that folder gets the VirusTotal links of the two new files. The copy of README.txt inside the setup points to the release page for the VirusTotal results, because the setup cannot hold its own scan link.
The setup holds the app as it is built on this PC, the Microsoft packages it needs and the tray icons program. Another Windows 10 or 11 PC installs it in about a minute, with no download and no build.
Each run creates a throwaway signing certificate, signs a copy of the app with it, puts its public part into the setup and deletes the certificate and its private key. The own signing certificate of the PC is never used, so no other package can ever be signed with the certificate that other PCs trust.
The setup asks for administrator approval once, to add that certificate to Trusted People. It installs the app for the signed-in user.
A setup window offers two options. Both are on by default. "Add a Start menu shortcut" adds the Start menu folder FluentWeather-Plus with FluentWeather-Plus, FluentWeather-Plus tray icons, Read me and Uninstall FluentWeather-Plus. The setup removes the Start menu folder FluentWeather of earlier versions. "Start the tray icons when Windows starts" adds a Startup item for the tray icons. The setup always adds the entry "FluentWeather-Plus with tray icons" in Settings > Apps.
A newer setup installs over an older one, keeps saved locations and settings and replaces the older certificate.
The uninstaller asks for administrator approval first. If the approval is declined, it removes nothing. It removes the app, the tray icons, the Start menu folder, the Startup item, the Settings > Apps entry, the tray settings and the certificate. Windows deletes the install folder at the next restart.
The setup and the uninstaller stop on a PC that has the build setup, so they never change that PC.
Each PC compiles both programs from FluentWeatherDirect.cs with the C# compiler that ships with Windows. Both programs do all their work through Windows interfaces. They start no PowerShell and no command prompt.


Part 2. Features of the original FluentWeather 2.1.0 by Gabboxl
===============================================================

FluentWeather 2.1.0 by Gabboxl is a weather app for Windows 10 and Windows 11 in the Fluent Design style. FluentWeather-Plus keeps all of its features. Some of them work differently in FluentWeather-Plus, as Part 1 describes.

The original app has these features.

The weather data comes from weather.com.
A search box finds a place by name and suggests matching places while typing.
On the first start a welcome window asks for a place.
The main area shows the place name, a large weather icon, the temperature, the conditions and the feels like temperature.
A line shows the date and time of the last update.
A refresh button loads the weather again.
Six detail cards show the wind speed and direction, the humidity, the pressure, the visibility, the dew point and the UV index with its category.
The daily forecast covers 10 days. Each day button shows the day name, a weather icon, the high and low temperatures and the chance of precipitation.
Clicking a day button shows the hourly forecast and the weather insights of that day.
The hourly forecast shows the hour, a weather icon, the temperature, the conditions and the chance of precipitation for each hour.
Clicking an hour opens a "Detailed data" panel with the feels like temperature, the wind and gusts, the humidity, the UV index, the cloud cover, the rain amount and the pressure.
"Weather Insights" shows a day summary and a night summary of the forecast.
The Sun insight shows the sunrise and sunset times.
The Moon insight shows the moonrise and moonset times and the lunar phase.
Four indices rate the conditions for running, driving, dry skin and watering. Each has a value and a category.
A background photo matches the current weather and fades in when the weather changes. The photos are under the Unsplash License.
On Windows 10 a Start menu live tile shows the place, the temperature and the conditions. The lock screen can show the same details. A background task updates the tile every 15 minutes and when the network changes.
After an update a "What's new" window shows the changes of the new version.
The app is available in 33 languages. Volunteers translate it on Crowdin.
The app is free software under the GNU General Public License version 3.

The settings panel under the gear button has these settings.

Units chooses Metric, Imperial or Hybrid.
Time format chooses 24 hours or 12 hours.
Acrylic effects turns the see-through acrylic surfaces on or off.
Background image turns the weather photo on or off.
Auto refresh loads the weather again every 5, 10, 15 or 30 minutes or every hour, or never.
The credits link to the designer whose work inspired the look, to the GitHub project and to the translation project.
